# Crit 10 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

This week didn't have a major breakthrough like the previous two. What it did
have was the game's systems coming together into something more and more
complete. With little experience making games, I've largely been taking it one
step at a time; this week, I think every important framework and direction in
the game got settled. I'm not sure that counts as a breakthrough, but it does
feel like a milestone, a clear dividing line.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

As for using the AI, honestly, I can't find much to fault. My main workflow
now is to spend ten-odd minutes settling a plan with it, then leave it an hour
or two to carry it out on its own. I suppose you could say I'm letting go of
it more and more. I've also mostly stopped editing the harness myself.
Instead, I wrote a set of rules that has the AI maintain its own handoff
documents and write my rules into them. Some problems with this approach are
already starting to show: a few offhand remarks of mine got turned into hard
rules. But the convenience it brings is something I can't really quantify,
because I've never objectively compared working with a system like this
against working without one. Subjectively, though, it takes a lot off my
mind, and the AI now understands what I mean and what I want quite well.
